## 1. Literature Search Strategy & Verification Methodology

To ensure evidence integrity and avoid fabricated or unverified citations, this literature review follows the protocol established in `ai-ml-research-assistant` and `scientific-thinking-literature-review`:

- **Primary Databases & Proceedings:** IEEE Xplore, CVF Open Access (CVPR, ICCV, WACV), NeurIPS Proceedings, Interspeech (ISCA Archive), ICASSP, ACM Multimedia, arXiv, and Hugging Face Papers.
- **Verification Levels:**
  - `[Full-Text Verified]`: The published PDF or conference paper was examined directly for exact architectures, evaluation protocols, and reported numbers.
  - `[Author Code Verified]`: Official open-source repositories were cloned or inspected for reproducible scripts and model weights.
  - `[Preprint / Peer-Reviewed]`: Clearly distinguished in all citation tables.
- **Cross-Evaluation Rule:** Results evaluated under different datasets, compression rates (e.g., FF++ raw vs. c23 vs. c40), or evaluation splits are never directly equated or merged.

## 2. Phase 1: Image Deepfake & Synthetic Image Detection

### 2.1 Thematic Overview
Image manipulation research has progressed through three primary generations:
1. **First Generation (2017–2020): Artifact & Blending Forensics.** Early works focused on identifying boundary artifacts created during Poisson blending in face-swapping pipelines (e.g., Face X-ray) or spatial pooling anomalies (MesoNet, early Xception).
2. **Second Generation (2020–2022): GAN Frequency Fingerprints.** Researchers demonstrated that transposed convolutions and upsampling in GAN architectures (ProGAN, StyleGAN) leave persistent high-frequency spectral artifacts visible in the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Fourier domains (Wang et al., Frank et al., F3Net).
3. **Third Generation (2022–2026): Diffusion & Universal Generalization.** Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), Stable Diffusion, and continuous-space generators eliminate discrete grid-upsampling artifacts. Detectors have shifted toward foundation vision-language features (CLIP-based probes by Ojha et al.), diffusion reconstruction error (DIRE), and unified open-source benchmarks (DeepfakeBench).

## 3. Phase 2: Audio Deepfake & Speech Synthesis Detection

### 3.1 Thematic Overview
Audio deepfake detection centers on discerning synthetic acoustic traces produced by neural vocoders (HiFi-GAN, WaveGlow, MelGAN) and speech generation models (TTS, Voice Conversion, Zero-Shot Cloners):
1. **Frontend Representations:** Traditional spectral frontends (Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients - LFCC, Constant Q Transform - CQT) capture short-term phase and spectral anomalies but struggle when audio is compressed with Opus, AAC, or MP3 codecs.
2. **End-to-End Raw Waveform Models:** Architectures like RawNet2 and AASIST operate directly on raw audio waveforms using learnable sinc-filters and spectro-temporal graph attention networks, achieving ultra-low Equal Error Rates (EER < 1%) on clean, uncompressed benchmarks.
3. **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Representations:** Foundation speech encoders (Wav2Vec 2.0, WavLM, OpenAI Whisper) pretrained on tens of thousands of hours of speech capture rich phonemic and acoustic invariants. Pretrained Whisper features (Interspeech 2023) dramatically outperform previous systems in unconstrained "In-The-Wild" settings.

## 4. Phase 3: Multimodal (Audio-Visual) Deepfake Detection

### 4.1 Thematic Overview
Multimodal deepfakes exhibit complex manipulation patterns: sometimes only the face is swapped (visual fake, real audio), sometimes the speech is cloned (real video, synthetic audio), and sometimes both are forged with lip-synchronization tools like Wav2Lip.
1. **Unimodal Fallacy:** Simply concatenating unimodal visual and audio vectors leads to the "modality dominance" problem: the classifier typically overfits to high-dimensional visual features and completely ignores audio-visual desynchronization.
2. **Phoneme-Viseme & Temporal Coherence:** Authentic humans display strict physical synchrony between the acoustic onset of bilabial consonants (/p/, /b/, /m/) and physical lip closure. Manipulated speech videos regularly violate this natural bio-physical correlation (LipForensics, AVOiD-DF).
3. **Self-Supervised Audio-Visual Anomaly Detection:** Rather than training on specific fake generators, modern systems (Feng et al., CVPR 2023) train autoregressive models exclusively on authentic videos to learn natural multi-modal joint distributions. Forgeries are detected as statistical anomalies.

## 5. Synthesis: Common Pitfalls & Methodological Traps

1. **The Intra-Dataset Inflation Trap:** Models reporting >99% AUC on FaceForensics++ c0/c23 almost always drop to 65%–75% when tested on Celeb-DF, WildDeepfake, or social media uploads. Research must report **cross-dataset generalization** as the primary benchmark.
2. **Unbalanced Metric Misrepresentation:** In real-world deployment, real images outnumber fake images 100:1. Relying solely on ROC-AUC hides high False Alarm Rates (FAR). We mandate reporting **Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC)**, **False Positive Rate at 95% Recall (FPR95)**, and **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**.
3. **Modality Imbalance in Multimodal Fusion:** Training a standard feed-forward layer on concatenated video and audio embeddings results in visual dominance. Multimodal architectures must employ **explicit cross-modal attention** or **decoupled late decision fusion** to preserve unimodal sensitivity.
